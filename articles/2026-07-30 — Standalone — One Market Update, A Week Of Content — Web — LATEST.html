One Market Update, A Week Of Content

Most agents post nothing because writing the post is the job they hate.

LC
Leo Chen
AI Money Machine · 4 min read

You know you should be posting. Market update, a story about a client, something. You have known this for two years. You have posted four times.

It is not that you do not have anything to say. You closed three deals last month and could talk for twenty minutes about any of them. It is that turning what you know into a paragraph, then a caption, then a text you would actually send a client, feels like homework assigned by nobody, due whenever you get around to it. So you never get around to it.

What one pull actually turns into

Here is the system running on my own list right now, live, every other week, without me touching it.

I pull the local numbers, not a syndicated report nobody in my market recognizes. New listings, price changes, days on market, what actually moved. That pull happens the night before, and by morning it has already become three separate things: a market read written in my voice for the email, a shorter version cut for whichever social platform I am using that week, and a one-line version short enough to paste into a text to a past client who asked "how's the market."

By 7:31 the email is in inboxes. I did not sit down and write any of it.

That is the whole trick. One pull, three shapes. The email does the thinking. The social post and the text just borrow it.

The part that makes agents skeptical

This sounds like a robot pretending to have opinions about the market. It is not. The read is built from real local numbers pulled fresh, not a template with your city's name swapped in, and it is written the way you would say it, not the way a report writes it. You are not publishing something you did not check. You are publishing something you did not have to type.

Think of it as three coffee machines. Drip is you, writing it yourself, which is why it stopped happening. Pod is a template you fill in every time, which is faster but still homework you have to sit down and do. The barista machine is what runs on my list: numbers go in, three finished pieces of content come out, and the only thing that changes week to week is the market.

You are not asking a chatbot to write you a caption. You are training an employee whose one job is to notice the market moved and tell your list about it, every two weeks, without a reminder from you.

Takes two minutes. Costs nothing.

Open Claude Cowork and start a new project for your business. Give it three facts: your market, your niche, and three words that describe how you talk.

Pull whatever local market numbers you have handy, even three data points. Ask it to write one short paragraph in your voice explaining what changed and why it matters to someone selling this month.

Read it back. If it sounds like something you would actually send, you just wrote the piece of content that has been sitting unwritten for two years.

Watch what changes when posting stops being the thing you mean to do and becomes the thing that already happened by the time you wake up.
