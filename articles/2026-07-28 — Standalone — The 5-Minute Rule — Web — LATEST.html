The 5-Minute Rule

The lead who waits thirty minutes for your reply already talked to somebody else — and no amount of hustle fixes a coverage problem.

LC
Leo Chen
AI Money Machine · 4 min read

A portal lead comes in at 2:14 on a Tuesday. You are standing in someone's kitchen talking about countertops. You see the notification. You think, I'll answer that in the car.

You answer it at 4:40. She replied to three agents at 2:15 and toured a house with one of them Saturday.

That is not a hustle problem. You were working. It is a coverage problem, and no amount of discipline fixes it, because the hours you are most likely to get a lead are the exact hours you are least able to answer one.

The numbers on this are old and brutal. The Lead Response Management study out of MIT put a figure on it back in 2007: the odds of qualifying a lead drop by twenty-one times between a five minute response and a thirty minute one. Not twenty-one percent. Twenty-one times. From five minutes to ten, it already drops five-fold.

Every agent knows speed matters. Nobody has a plan for being fast at 2:14 while holding a tape measure.

What answering the door actually looks like

Here is the system I run on my own rental listings, and it is the same shape for buyer leads.

A lead hits the portal. Inside a couple of minutes, before I have seen it, there is a reply sitting in her inbox written in my voice. It thanks her for the specific property she asked about, and it asks for the two things I need to move: her phone number and the best time to reach her.

How It Runs
1
Portal lead lands, phone still in your pocket
2
Reply sent in under 2 minutes, in your voice, asking for number and best time
3
She answers — tagged in both CRMs, added to the call queue
4
She's not interested — marked not qualified, drops off your list
It reads your CRM the same way you do, so nothing changes by hand.

By the time I sit down in the car, I am not reading forty unread portal notifications trying to work out who is real. I am looking at a short list of people who answered a question, with their number already attached.

Nothing about that is a chatbot conversation. It is a front desk.

The person at a front desk does not close anybody. They greet, they get the number, they route. That is the entire job, and it is the job you are currently doing yourself, badly, from a kitchen.

The part that makes agents nervous

Most agents hear "AI answers my leads" and picture a bot pretending to be them for six exchanges and saying something stupid about a school district.

That is not this. Set the scope small and the risk goes with it. One reply. Confirm the property, ask for the number and a good time, stop. It never quotes a price it should not, never promises a showing you have not confirmed, never argues. Everything past the handoff is you.

You are not asking a chatbot for one follow-up text. You are training an employee to answer your front door, every time somebody knocks, for the rest of your career.

Takes two minutes. Costs nothing.

Open Claude Cowork and start a new project for your business. Give it three facts: your market, your niche, and three words that describe how you talk.

Then paste in the last portal inquiry you got and the reply you actually sent. Ask it to write the same reply in your voice, in under sixty words, ending with a request for a phone number and a good time to call.

Read what comes back. If it sounds like you, you have just written the first line of a front desk that never goes to a showing.

Watch what changes when the wait between "she asked" and "somebody answered" stops depending on where you are standing.
